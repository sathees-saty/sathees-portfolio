Case Study 04

B2B eCommerce Modernization

🌐 Domain: B2B eCommerce / Digital Commerce
💼 Role: Program / Project Manager
🔄 Transformation: Monolith → Microservices
☁️ Platform: Cloud-Native Digital Commerce

Business Challenge

The organization was operating a legacy B2B eCommerce platform built on a tightly coupled monolithic architecture. The platform had increasing complexity, lengthy release cycles, limited scalability and strong dependencies between commerce, product, pricing, order and SAP integration capabilities. The modernization program focused on transforming the platform into a scalable, modular and cloud-ready microservices architecture while maintaining business continuity.

Program Scope

  • Modernization of the existing B2B eCommerce platform from monolithic architecture to microservices.
  • Decomposition of tightly coupled business capabilities into independently deployable services.
  • Modernization of customer, product, catalog, pricing, cart and order capabilities.
  • Integration with SAP ERP and enterprise business systems.
  • API modernization and enterprise integration.
  • Cloud adoption and cloud-native deployment.
  • DevOps and CI/CD modernization.
  • Data migration and synchronization.
  • Performance, scalability and availability improvement.
  • Product release and operational transition.

Transformation Objectives

  • Reduce dependency on the legacy monolithic architecture.
  • Enable independent development and deployment of business capabilities.
  • Improve scalability and platform performance.
  • Accelerate product release cycles.
  • Enable cloud-native deployment and operations.
  • Improve integration with SAP and enterprise systems.
  • Establish reusable APIs and services.
  • Create a foundation for future digital commerce capabilities.

Transformation Strategy

The modernization adopted an incremental transformation strategy rather than a high-risk big-bang replacement. Business capabilities were progressively separated from the monolith and implemented as independently deployable services.

  • Assessed the existing monolithic architecture and business capability dependencies.
  • Identified candidate capabilities for microservices decomposition.
  • Prioritized services based on business value, technical complexity and dependency risk.
  • Defined APIs and service boundaries.
  • Established coexistence between legacy functionality and new microservices.
  • Progressively migrated functionality while maintaining business operations.

Technology Architecture

The target architecture follows a modular, API-first and cloud-native approach. Core B2B commerce capabilities are separated into independently deployable services and integrated with SAP and enterprise systems through APIs and integration services.

Architecture Components

  • Customer Management: B2B customer profiles, accounts, organizations, contacts and account relationships.
  • Product & Catalog: Product information, catalog hierarchy, categories and product availability.
  • Pricing: Customer-specific pricing, contracts, discounts and pricing rules.
  • Cart & Checkout: Shopping cart, checkout, validation and order submission.
  • Order Management: Order creation, status tracking, fulfillment and order lifecycle management.
  • Payment: Payment processing and payment-provider integration.
  • API Gateway: Secure API exposure, routing, authentication and traffic management.
  • Integration Layer: APIs and integration services connecting eCommerce, SAP and enterprise systems.
  • Data Platform: Service-specific data stores, synchronization, caching and analytics.
  • Observability: Application monitoring, logging, alerting, performance and operational visibility.

SAP & Enterprise Integration

The B2B eCommerce platform integrates with SAP and enterprise systems to support end-to-end business processes.

  • SAP S/4HANA integration for enterprise customer and business transactions.
  • Product and material master integration.
  • Customer and account synchronization.
  • Pricing and condition integration.
  • Inventory and availability integration.
  • Order creation and order status integration.
  • Invoice and fulfillment integration.
  • API and enterprise integration services.

Product Lifecycle

The product lifecycle was managed from business vision and discovery through product development, release, production stabilization and continuous improvement.

01 — Product Discovery

  • Identified customer and business pain points.
  • Defined product vision and strategic objectives.
  • Conducted stakeholder and customer discovery.
  • Identified business capabilities and modernization opportunities.
  • Defined product roadmap and transformation priorities.

02 — Requirements & Product Backlog

  • Defined functional and non-functional requirements.
  • Created epics, features and user stories.
  • Prioritized backlog based on business value, dependencies and technical complexity.
  • Defined acceptance criteria and Definition of Done.
  • Maintained product roadmap and release priorities.

03 — Architecture & Design

  • Defined microservices boundaries and domain responsibilities.
  • Designed API contracts and integration patterns.
  • Defined cloud deployment architecture.
  • Established security and authentication architecture.
  • Defined data migration and synchronization approach.

04 — Development

  • Coordinated development across multiple product and engineering teams.
  • Managed microservices development and API implementation.
  • Coordinated SAP and enterprise integrations.
  • Managed technical dependencies and cross-team integration.
  • Monitored quality, scope, schedule and delivery risks.

05 — Testing & Validation

  • Functional and integration testing.
  • API and end-to-end testing.
  • Performance and scalability testing.
  • Security testing.
  • User acceptance testing.
  • Business process validation across eCommerce and SAP.

06 — Release & Deployment

  • Established release planning and deployment governance.
  • Coordinated application, infrastructure and integration releases.
  • Managed production readiness and Go-Live checkpoints.
  • Coordinated deployment and rollback planning.
  • Managed stakeholder communication throughout production transition.

07 — Hypercare & Stabilization

  • Established enhanced production support.
  • Monitored platform performance and transaction health.
  • Coordinated rapid defect and incident resolution.
  • Monitored integration and SAP transaction issues.
  • Transitioned stabilized capabilities to BAU operations.

08 — Continuous Improvement

  • Reviewed product performance and customer feedback.
  • Identified platform optimization opportunities.
  • Prioritized new product capabilities.
  • Improved scalability, reliability and operational efficiency.
  • Maintained the product roadmap for future modernization initiatives.

Program Management Focus

  • Transformation roadmap and program governance.
  • Product roadmap and release management.

  • Stakeholder and executive communication.
  • RAID and dependency management.
  • Architecture and technology governance.
  • Vendor and partner management.
  • Financial, resource and delivery planning.
  • Production readiness and operational transition.

Technology Landscape

Transformation Outcome
Established a scalable, modular and cloud-ready B2B eCommerce architecture by progressively transforming the legacy monolith into independently deployable microservices while maintaining integration with SAP and enterprise systems.
Program Management Takeaway: Monolith-to-microservices transformation is not simply a technology migration. Successful modernization requires business capability decomposition, product roadmap management, architecture governance, API strategy, data management, release planning and continuous stakeholder alignment.
